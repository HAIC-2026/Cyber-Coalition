# Project Scope and Demonstration Boundaries

**Project:** HAIC 2026 Cyber Coalition, DHS Enhanced ESF-6 planning challenge  
**Status:** Working draft for review; no agency approval implied.

## Objective

Demonstrate an evidence-traceable, privacy-preserving planning workflow for mass care and related ESF-6 needs. The project is intended to help planners connect public, aggregate information to questions about needs, resources, roles, and decisions. It does not replace emergency managers or agency plans.

## Two geographic scopes

| Scope | What it means | What the team may claim |
|---|---|---|
| Statewide framework | The design accounts for all Hawaiʻi counties/islands and identifies how coverage and missingness would be reported. | Statewide design intent only until all required sources and measures have been verified. |
| Kapolei / Leeward Oʻahu pilot | One fictional Category 5 hurricane wind-stress tabletop in the City and County of Honolulu. | A method demonstration for this bounded scenario only. |

The written DHS challenge PDF has been reviewed. Its scope and measurable-goal sections (pp. 5–7) specify four core hazards: hurricane, wildfire, tsunami, and flood. The measurable goal is hazard-specific AFN vulnerability maps for every county, with a target of 100% of census tracts. Section 4 separately includes earthquake in a list of hazard types, and the kickoff transcript also mentions earthquake. To keep the committed baseline aligned with the explicit four-hazard scope and metric wording, this project uses the four listed hazards; earthquake remains an unresolved interpretation question or possible later extension, not a fifth committed deliverable. Record the ambiguity in submission notes if no organizer clarification is received.

## Scenario assumptions

- Fictional Category 5 maximum sustained wind scenario; NHC scale threshold is at least 157 mph.
- Category identifies wind category only. Surge, rainfall flooding, tornadoes, outage, road access, timing, footprint, and participation are separate scenario assumptions.
- The scenario is a tabletop and not a real event, forecast, alert, evacuation instruction, or operational recommendation.
- Synthetic values may be used to exercise workflow behavior. They must be labeled synthetic and kept separate from observations or official data.
- County-led decisions and a simulated request for State support are modeled for demonstration. The exact process must be checked against current official plans.

## Planning needs lens

Use CMIST to organize aggregate planning considerations: Communication, Maintaining Health, Independence, Safety and Support, and Transportation. These dimensions describe planning needs, not individual classifications. Do not infer a person's diagnosis, eligibility, address, or service requirement from aggregate data.

## Decision authority

The interface can prepare options, source-linked summaries, unresolved questions, and simulated request materials. An authorized human must review the evidence and make any real decision. The prototype cannot issue alerts or orders, determine individual eligibility, open or close shelters, dispatch personnel, commit a partner's resources, or connect to WebEOC or other live response systems.

## Planning method

1. Objective and call to action.
2. Setting and backstory.
3. Stakeholders, roles, and data.
4. Evidence-supported themes and potential root causes.
5. Feasible scenarios and decision points.
6. Proposed plan and workflow.

For each conclusion, record whether it is a source finding, an inference, a planning assumption, a proposed requirement, or an open question.
