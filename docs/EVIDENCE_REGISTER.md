## HI-EOP and SESF #6 Mass Care Annex reviewed (2026-11-19)

This responds to the user's request to use Hawaiʻi's actual emergency management framework — not an invented one — to solidify the project's county-to-state coordination pathway. The HI-EMA "Emergency Operations Plan and Annexes" index page (`https://dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/emergency-operations-plan-and-annexes/`) was fetched directly (HTTP 200) and its PDF links extracted. Two documents were downloaded and read in full and are the basis for the claims below; both are primary, authoritative sources (Evidence Class B/C in the user's evidentiary framework — official state emergency-management publications), not AI-generated or secondary summaries.

| Document | Status | Source |
|---|---|---|
| State of Hawaiʻi Emergency Operations Plan: Base Plan (HI-EOP) | Signed by Governor Josh Green, Feb 17, 2026; HIEMA Administrator James DS. Barros and HI-DOD Director MG Stephen F. Logan co-signed Feb 10–11, 2026. Record of Approval shows this is the 5th approved revision ("Revision & Re-signature"), supersedes a 2022 edition. Directed by HRS Chapter 127A. | `https://dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/files/2026/03/HI-EOP-Base-Plan-Final-Signed.pdf` |
| SESF #6 Mass Care, Emergency Assistance, Housing & Human Services Annex | Dated September 2025; signed by HIEMA Administrator James DS. Barros and SESF #6 Mass Care EMO Joseph Campos II, Oct 2, 2025. | `https://dod.hawaii.gov/hiema/files/2025/10/SESF6-Annex_30SEP25.pdf` |

**Fact, high confidence — the documented county-to-state coordination procedure (HI-EOP §4.4, §5.3–5.4; SESF #6 "Scope," "Direction, Control, and Coordination"):**

1. **Incident level** — an on-scene Incident Commander manages most incidents with county public-safety resources (HI-EOP §4.4.1).
2. **County EOC level** — when size/complexity exceeds on-scene capacity, the county activates its local EOC (partial or full) under its own emergency operations plan. The mayor *may* declare a local state of emergency by proclamation (HRS §127A-14(b), §127A-13); a proclamation is not required to request state assistance, and a proclamation does not automatically mean state assistance is requested (HI-EOP §4.4.2, §4.4.2.1).
3. **State EOC (SEOC) level** — "the emergency management agency of the impacted jurisdiction requests assistance through the SEOC when local resources are exhausted or the county does not possess the required capability. Upon receipt and verification of the county's request for help, HIEMA tasks the appropriate state agency with providing the requested resources, services, or information. These assignments are generally coordinated through the SESF structure" (HI-EOP §4.4.3, quoted). State agencies may also request SEOC assistance via their Emergency Management Officer through WebEOC.
4. **Mechanics of the request (HI-EOP §5.3 "Requests for Assistance and Information," Figure 22):** the county (or state department) submits a Request for Assistance (RFA) or Request for Information (RFI) through the online WebEOC system. A Desk/RFA Manager in the SEOC Operations Section reviews and assigns it to the tasked SESF entity ("ESF Planning"), which researches and answers RFIs directly, or plans/coordinates resources for RFAs and routes them through Ops Approval before the tasked SESF executes (sourced → deployed/on-site → demobilized → completed). If the assigned SESF cannot fulfill the request, it is reassigned to another SESF or forwarded to the Logistics and Resource Support Section (SESF #7). If it cannot be met with in-state resources: the Governor's declared state of emergency allows fulfillment via the Emergency Management Assistance Compact (interstate mutual aid, HI-EOP §8.2.2); a presidential (Stafford Act) disaster declaration allows forwarding to FEMA (HI-EOP §4.4.4).
5. **SEOC coordination with counties (HI-EOP §5.4):** a County Desk position is staffed in the SEOC Operations Section per impacted county to maintain a direct line with that county's EOC; the WebEOC Event Log is the primary shared situational-awareness tool. An Emergency Management Assistance Team (EMAT) of HIEMA staff/SERT personnel may be requested by county EOCs via WebEOC when county capabilities are overwhelmed.
6. **SESF #6 specifically (SESF #6 Annex, "Direction, Control, and Coordination," "Scope"):** HIEMA and the Hawaiʻi Department of Human Services are the SESF #6 primary agencies. The American Red Cross is the primary agency for temporary shelter operations, the Salvation Army for feeding operations, and Hawaiʻi Voluntary Organizations Active in Disaster (HVOAD) for mass-care service delivery generally. DHS is "the primary interface with the [SEOC Operations Section Chief] and the member agencies of SESF #6.
